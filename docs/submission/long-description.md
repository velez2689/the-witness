A medical biller spends 25 minutes and $13.80 on one phone call asking a payer why a claim was denied (CAQH Index, 2024 edition, 2023 data year). The answer is spoken, unrecorded on the provider's side, and gone the moment they hang up. Weeks later a different representative gives a different answer, and nothing notices. About 19 percent of in-network claims are denied and under 1 percent of those denials are appealed (KFF, March 2026). The gap is not merit. It is evidence: appealing means reconstructing what the payer said, and nobody can.

We don't predict what the payer will pay. We record what the payer said.

The Witness rides along on the call. Every statement the payer representative makes becomes a timestamped, quotable row in an append-only, hash-chained ledger keyed to the claim. Five kinds of contradiction are detected against earlier calls on the same claim, in real time, and a refusal (the payer names a category but withholds the value) is recorded as a row of its own: the absence is the evidence. In Mode A the Witness conducts the call itself. It discloses that it is an AI assistant and that the call is recorded, challenges the contradiction on the recorded line ("on July eighth you told us no prior authorization; today you're saying timely filing, which one is on the claim?"), reads reference numbers back, and refuses to hang up until every required field is captured or refused. The appeal packet quotes the transcript with rep, badge, reference number, date and offset, and carries a hash anyone can recompute.

Why AssemblyAI: spoken alphanumerics are the hard case, and reference numbers, member IDs and badge numbers are exactly that. The claim's own identifiers are seeded as keyterms before the call, and a reference number that lands unconfirmed triggers a spoken read-back instead of a guess. The Rep's audio goes to Streaming STT v3, verbatim and never cleaned, because the self-correction a rep makes mid-sentence is often the contradiction. The Witness speaks through the Voice Agent API, which is used as a mouth only: it is never sent audio, never calls tools and never writes the ledger, by choice. Every sentence it speaks is assembled by our code from statement rows, and a self-check compares every number it actually says against the record. No accuracy percentage is claimed anywhere; confidence is shown as a state.
